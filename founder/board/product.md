# Product lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The job is concrete and felt. A dirty or broken aircon is a problem the customer notices, and "cold air again, clean unit, no mess" is an outcome they can judge in one visit.
- The service happens at the customer's door, so the whole experience (booking, arrival, work, clean-up, receipt, follow-up) can be designed and owned end to end by one small team.
- Small scope is possible. Starting as one technician in one town is the right size to get the experience right before adding anything.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No "one thing."** Residential and small business, cleaning and repair, maybe parts, refrigerant and installs: this is several businesses at once. Without a cut, there is nothing to be brilliant at, and the pitch cannot fit in one sentence.
2. **The experience lives in someone else's hands.** The certified technician is the product the customer actually meets, yet the deal with them is undefined. The handoff from founder (booking, promises) to technician (the visit) is the seam where quality, punctuality and trust break, and there is no stated standard for what a visit looks like.
3. **No designed experience yet.** No name, no price, no channel, no difference stated. Nothing describes the first five minutes for the customer, so there is nothing to work back from, and the risk is a generic service that competes only on availability.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, what does a customer get from you that they cannot get from the next technician they find?
- Walk me through one visit, minute by minute: how they book, who shows up, what the technician does before touching the unit, how they leave the room, what the customer receives after.
- What will you say no to in year one: repairs, business customers, parts, installs?
- What does the technician agree to on quality and conduct, and what happens if they leave?
- How will you check the work nobody sees (inside the unit, drainage, leaks) is done right every time?
- Open unknowns to resolve, not assume: what buyers will pay, what local competitors charge and how they perform, and the starting budget.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Pick one customer (households or small businesses) and one service (cleaning is the natural start) and write the offer in one sentence.
2. Write a one-page visit standard covering booking, arrival, protection of the room, the cleaning steps, before/after proof, clean-up, receipt and a follow-up message.
3. Put the technician arrangement in writing, including the visit standard and what happens if they stop working with the business.
4. Run a small set of real visits against the standard and bring back what customers said before expanding scope.
