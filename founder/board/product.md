# Product lens
Score: 5 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- There is a clear candidate for the one thing: salon or wedding-grade nail design, done at home.
- Home service removes travel, waiting and parking, a better moment for a bride and entourage getting ready together.
- The founder's strength in operations and workflows fits the hardest part of a home service: scheduling, kits, hygiene routines and follow-up.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The craft is unowned.** Nobody has said who does the nails. The technician's hands are the product. If the work is not visibly mall-salon grade, the promise fails.
2. **No focus.** The menu covers design, cleaning, trims, cuticles, mani and pedi, for brides, professionals and anyone who stays home. That is a salon menu, not a product, and entry services can crowd out the design work.
3. **Seams in the home experience.** Booking channel is undecided, so the first touchpoint does not exist yet. Lighting, setup, sanitation, mess, lateness, gel curing and a fix if a nail chips are all moments where a home visit can feel worse than a salon.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, what is this?
- Who is doing the nails, and can we see ten finished sets of their work?
- Describe the first five minutes after the tech arrives. What gets set up, where, and how long does it take?
- What would you cut from the menu first?
- What happens when a nail chips two days before a wedding?
- Open questions for other skills: what mall salons and home-service rivals in east Metro Manila actually charge (/founder-competitors), whether brides will pay for home design and how they book today (/founder-consumer), what each visit costs in materials, travel and tech time (/founder-cfo), and the price point (/founder-pricing). The ₱3,000 reference is unchecked.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Name the technician (founder or hire) and show a portfolio that matches mall-salon or bridal quality.
2. Narrow the launch to one thing: nail design at home, led by bridal and event bookings. Basic care only as an add-on to a design booking.
3. Write the full visit as a checklist from first message to follow-up: booking, confirmation, arrival window, setup kit, sanitation, finish photo, aftercare, fix policy. Run five test visits before charging full price.
4. Bring back price and cost per visit from the skills named above.
