# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- The need is real and recurring. Aircons need cleaning on a regular basis, so one customer can come back many times instead of buying once.
- Small geography. Cainta is a small market, and this lens likes a small market that can be dominated first. A single town gives a clear target for "own it first".
- Low engineering risk. The service is proven, so the open question is whether anyone needs this particular provider, not whether the service works.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No secret and no 10x edge.** Cleaning and repair is incremental at best. The brief names nothing proprietary, no breakthrough in speed, quality or price, and no point of difference. Nothing stops a copycat in month six, and the business is a copycat on day one.
2. **The know-how belongs to someone else.** The certified technician is the only person who can deliver the service, and the deal with them is undefined. If they leave, take customers, or start their own shop, the business has nothing left. The know-how that should be proprietary sits outside the company.
3. **No distribution plan.** How customers will find the business is listed as unknown. In a crowded local service market, getting customers is the whole game, and right now there is no answer for where the first 100 come from or what each one costs.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who else cleans and repairs aircons within reach of a Cainta household today, and what do buyers dislike about them? (Open: competitors not yet researched.)
- What do you know about this market that others do not?
- What is the smallest segment you could win most of first: one subdivision, one building type, small shops on one road?
- How do the first 100 customers arrive, and what does each one cost to win?
- What is the technician's deal, and what stops them from going independent with your customer list?
- What could build lasting strength over ten years: a local brand, route density (scale), maintenance contracts that lock in repeat work, or referrals?
- Why now? What has changed that makes this the right moment?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable at PASS. What would move this to FUND IF:
- A named beachhead (for example, small businesses or particular subdivisions in Cainta) with a credible plan to win a large share of it.
- A written technician agreement that ties the technician to the company (equity, profit share, non-solicit) and a plan to certify a second technician.
- One concrete, testable advantage that buyers care about, such as recurring maintenance contracts, guaranteed response times, or a lead channel competitors do not use.
- A tested acquisition channel with a known cost per customer.
