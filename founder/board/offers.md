# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Recurring, unavoidable need.** Aircon units need cleaning on a cycle, and when one breaks the customer wants it fixed now. Repeat demand and urgency come built in, so the business does not have to invent scarcity.
- **Buyers who can afford it.** A household or shop that owns an aircon has already spent money on cooling. Whether the market is growing is an open question.
- **Easy to find.** The service area is one town, and every unit owner is a possible customer. Reach is local and concrete.
- **Short time to result.** One visit gives a visible result: colder air, less noise, no dripping.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Nothing sets it apart, so it competes on price.** The brief has no name, no edge and no price. As described, it is "aircon cleaning, like everyone else." That makes it a commodity, and a commodity's only lever is being cheaper.
2. **Buyers have no reason to trust it.** The founder is not certified, the business has no reviews or track record, and the deal with the only technician is undefined. Buyers are letting a stranger into their home to work on an expensive appliance. With no proof, guarantee or brand, they have little reason to believe the job will go well.
3. **The whole offer depends on one person.** All delivery runs through one technician on undefined terms. If that person leaves, gets busy or starts taking customers directly, there is nothing left to sell.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which buyer first: households or small businesses? Their pain, budget and buying process differ. Who has the most pain and the most money?
- What is the dream outcome in the buyer's own words: lower power bills, no breakdowns in summer, no mess, no hassle?
- What guarantee could you safely offer? For example: a re-clean free if a problem comes back within a set period, or an on-time arrival promise.
- What does the buyer struggle with before, during and after a job (booking, mess, the price surprise, the next reminder)? Which of those can you solve cheaply and add to the offer?
- Could a maintenance plan (scheduled cleans each year, priority repairs) be the main product instead of one-off visits?
- If competitors charged double, would you still be the obvious choice? Why?
- What are the technician's terms, and what stops them from going around you?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Pick one buyer to start with, and define one named offer in one sentence that includes a guarantee.
2. Build the offer from the buyer's problems (booking, cleanup, follow-up reminders, written report) and price it on value, not on being below competitors. Test the price with real buyers before launch.
3. Put the technician deal in writing before launch, with a plan for a second technician.
4. Collect proof from the first jobs (reviews, before-and-after photos) before spending on marketing.
